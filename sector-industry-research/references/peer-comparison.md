# Peer Comparison

Use peer comparison to turn industry conclusions into listed-company read-through without overclaiming stock conclusions.

## Peer Set Construction

- Define geography, listing venue, business segment, customer/end-market exposure, and size threshold.
- Separate pure plays from conglomerates and suppliers/customers.
- Separate global leaders from local substitutes when market structure differs.
- Exclude companies only if the exclusion reason is stated.
- Use local-market peers for valuation when listing venue, liquidity, governance, or policy risk materially changes multiples.

## Comparison Dimensions

| Dimension | Use |
|---|---|
| Exposure | Segment revenue/profit share, customer mix, geography, end market |
| Quality | ROIC/ROE, gross margin, FCF conversion, R&D/product strength |
| Cycle sensitivity | Operating leverage, pricing beta, inventory risk, capex cycle |
| Balance sheet | Net cash/debt, refinancing, working capital, dilution risk |
| Execution | Delivery history, product validation, market share, customer wins |
| Valuation | P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/sales, P/B, NAV, FCF yield, sector-appropriate metrics |
| Implied assumption | What each peer multiple requires in growth, terminal margin, or sustainable ROE. See `../../references/implied-expectations.md` |
| Earnings quality | Cash conversion, accrual ratio, and working capital versus revenue growth, computed on the same basis across the set. See `../../references/earnings-quality-screens.md` |
| Capital allocation | Reinvestment rate, ROIIC on both bases, net dilution, and cumulative impairment rate, over the same window for every peer. Ranks who converted the same industry conditions into return. See `../../references/capital-allocation-record.md` |

## Classification

Use these labels as research shorthand, not buy/sell recommendations:

- Core profit pool: captures the largest or highest-quality industry profit pool with verified revenue, margin, and cash-flow linkage.
- Structural leader: high quality, durable share, pricing power, and strong balance sheet.
- Bottleneck supplier: benefits from scarce capacity, IP, equipment, license, infrastructure, data, distribution, or critical materials.
- Cyclical beneficiary: high earnings beta to an improving cycle, but weaker durability.
- Local substitute: benefits from localization or import substitution if adoption is verified.
- High-beta challenger: upside if execution works, but higher balance-sheet or dilution risk.
- Theme beta: mainly expresses narrative, policy, or risk appetite; require higher evidence before treating it as fundamental exposure.
- Value trap: low multiple but weak cycle position, governance, cash flow, or structural demand.
- Structural loser: the named other side of an industry shift, where share, pricing, or profit pool is transferring away. Route to `../../references/short-and-relative-value.md` before using any short-side language.
- Watchlist only: exposure is plausible, but data or valuation evidence is insufficient.

Most industry shifts have two sides. Naming only the beneficiary leaves half the
analysis unused, and the displaced peer is often the higher-conviction read because
the evidence is already visible in its numbers.

## Valuation Context

- Rank the peer set by implied assumption, not by multiple. Two names on the same P/E imply different growth when their ROIC differs, and the cheaper-looking name is often the more demanding one.
- Compare valuation to normalized earnings, not only peak or trough earnings.
- Label trailing versus forward multiples and GAAP versus adjusted metrics.
- For cyclicals, avoid calling a low P/E cheap at peak earnings. Solve for the mid-cycle EBITDA or long-run price the multiple implies and compare it with the prior cycle range.
- Compute the cash-conversion screens across the whole set before ranking on quality. A peer set where one name converts earnings to cash and another does not is not comparable on earnings multiples at all.
- For growth industries, bridge revenue growth to margin and cash flow before using sales multiples.
- For China/Hong Kong sectors, check policy, liquidity, shareholder return, and governance discounts.

## Relative Value Within The Peer Set

A peer table is the natural place to find a spread, because the legs already share
a driver by construction. When two peers share the demand driver but differ in
execution, balance sheet, or valuation, the more defensible read is often the
difference between them rather than a directional call on either.

State these before treating a peer gap as an opportunity:

- The shared driver, and evidence that both names actually move on it.
- Whether the valuation gap is at a historical extreme, and what has explained it historically.
- Whether a structural reason keeps the gap open: index membership, ownership structure, listing venue, liquidity, or governance.
- What would close the gap, with a date or an observable trigger.
- What breaks the spread rather than either leg.

Pair types, dual-listed share-class constraints, and holdco discounts are covered
in `../../references/short-and-relative-value.md`. A gap with no closing mechanism
is a description of the market, not a trade.

## Output Discipline

When ranking companies, separate:

- Industry exposure ranking.
- Business quality ranking, including the earnings-quality breach count per name.
- Valuation attractiveness, stated as the implied assumption rather than the multiple.
- Risk level and evidence gaps.
- Trade expression role: core profit pool, bottleneck supplier, cyclical beneficiary, theme beta, or watchlist only.

Do not collapse these into a single "best stock" unless company-level research gates are met.
