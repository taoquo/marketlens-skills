The band answers a narrow question: where does 55x sit in NVIDIA's own history? At the 51st percentile it is almost exactly at the median of its trailing 750-observation window. That is neither cheap nor expensive; it is unremarkable. The useful part is the slope: the rolling median has fallen from roughly 87x in late 2023 to 55x today, so the same absolute multiple now demands less than it did two years ago. The move was EPS-led: an illustrative TTM EPS path from $0.42 to $4.10 more than offset the multiple compression from 97x to 55x, so the price gain is not a re-rating. The band cannot tell you whether the earnings base or the implied growth is right; the reverse DCF still does that work.

Red Flags

  • The PE series is illustrative, not reconciled to filings. A synthetic PE series is Tier 3; confidence is capped at Medium. The decomposition uses an illustrative EPS path, not a reconciled filing series; it is arithmetic, not a second valuation model.
  • NVIDIA's earnings are cyclical and concentrated. A PE band on peak AI earnings can look cheap for the wrong reason.
  • The rolling median is falling. A lower multiple on a lower earnings base is not a cheaper company.
  • The band is raw PE, not rate-adjusted; a higher real-yield regime compresses the distribution. P20/P80 is a 60% central band, not a 68% band, and realized coverage is not computed. Do not call it a probability.

Decision Impact

  • Valuation layer: supports reasonable as a cross-check; cannot produce priced for perfection or not solvable alone.
  • Company layer: the 16/18 quality score and clean earnings read stand; the band does not change them.
  • Label: hold/watch. The band alone cannot support add-candidate watch or trim-review.
  • Confidence: Medium. The denominator is illustrative, and a 750-observation window is shorter than a full cycle.
  • Output: the figure is a context exhibit, not a target price or a probability of reversion.
