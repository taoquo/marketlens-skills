Investment Thesis

NVIDIA still earns a 14/18 quality score: the CUDA and Vera Rubin platform, $96.6B of FY26 free cash flow, and net cash. The score is two points lower than a year ago because cash conversion has weakened and the CEO transition exposed a governance gap. The price has reset hard: at $155.20 the market cap is about $3.74T, or 16.7x TTM EPS of $9.31, down from 34x a year ago. A reverse DCF at a 9% WACC now requires only a 6.2% ten-year NOPAT CAGR, and growth accounts for about 41% of enterprise value rather than 80%. That removes the valuation block. It does not remove the evidence block: Q3 operating cash flow covered only 0.74x net income, receivables grew 143% year on year, and the board has just reversed an 11-day CEO resignation. The label is hold/watch — the company is strong and the price is no longer demanding, but the earnings base and governance need proof.

Red Flags

Cash conversion
Q3 operating cash flow was $48.3B against $65.3B of net income, or 0.74x. Receivables rose to $81.0B from $33.4B a year earlier, a 143% increase against 103% revenue growth. DSO is 64 days versus 53 a year ago. The earnings base is growing faster than the cash.
Governance / key person
The CEO resigned on Nov 12 and was reinstated on Nov 23 after a board investigation and government consultation. The reversal removed the immediate uncertainty, but the disclosure record and the board's process remain a governance discount until the investigation closes.
AI capex concentration
About half of data center revenue comes from the largest hyperscalers, and the Q4 guide assumes zero China data center compute revenue. A cloud capex revision or a China export change hits revenue and multiple together.
Capital allocation
Q3 buybacks of $22.1B against a ~99M sequential diluted-share reduction imply roughly $220 per share, near the 52-week high; the stock is now $155. The company also deployed $58.1B into equity securities in the first nine months and agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9B. The read is adequate, not disciplined.

Decision Impact

Company layer 14/18 supports a long-term watch but no longer an unambiguous quality upgrade: moat and balance sheet remain strong, while earnings quality is watch (three screen breaches) and governance is the swing factor. The valuation layer has moved from priced for perfection to undemanding: 16.7x TTM, a 6.2% implied ten-year NOPAT CAGR, and 41% of EV in growth. That clears the old valuation block but does not create an add-candidate label, because capital allocation is only adequate and the evidence on cash conversion is unresolved. The label is hold/watch. An add-candidate watch needs two consecutive quarters of CFO/NI at or above 0.8 and a closed governance investigation, not a lower multiple.

What Would Change The View

Upgrade
Q4 FY27 gross margin at or above 72% and CFO/NI at or above 0.8 for two consecutive quarters; the board investigation closes without a new disclosure; FY28 EPS revisions stay positive while the multiple remains below 15x.
Downgrade
Q4 gross margin below 71%, data center growth below 60% year on year, CFO/NI below 0.6, or DSO above 70 days. A new CEO or board disclosure, or a China export change that removes more of the serviceable market, also downgrades.
Exit review
FCF/NI below 0.5 for two periods, a customer concentration above 60% from the top three, goodwill plus intangibles above 30% of invested capital after the Hugging Face deal, or a governance finding that changes control.
Evidence needed
A dated buyback price series, a customer-concentration table from the 10-Q, a maintenance-versus-growth capex split, and a normalized cash-conversion series. Without them, the allocation read stays adequate and confidence stays Medium.

Analyst Summary

NVIDIA is still a high-quality franchise, but the case has flipped. A year ago the price required a 22.5% ten-year NOPAT CAGR and 80% of EV in growth; at $155.20 the same model requires 6.2% and 41%. The multiple is no longer the problem. The problem is the quality of the earnings base: cash conversion is below 0.8, receivables are growing faster than revenue, and the CEO transition exposed a governance gap that the reinstatement does not fully close. The result is hold/watch, not add-candidate, until the cash evidence and the governance record improve. This is an illustrative case using public filings and a dated quote, not investment advice.
