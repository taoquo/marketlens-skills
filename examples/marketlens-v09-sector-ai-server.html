The AI server chain is still in structural expansion, but the profit pool is not evenly distributed. The same industry conditions produce different rankings depending on whether you sort by multiple, by the assumption the price requires, or by the return on incremental capital. The overlap is one segment: bottleneck suppliers with pricing power and ROIIC above WACC.

Red Flags

  • GPU EV/Sales implies a 167% steady-state EBIT margin. That is internally inconsistent; do not patch it with a growth story.
  • Cloud platform depreciation-life extension is an accounting estimate, not operating improvement.
  • Networking with-goodwill ROIIC is 0.72x the ex-goodwill basis, and 9% of cumulative M&A consideration is impaired.
  • Cloud reinvests 61% for a 9.4% ROIIC — expansion without a value spread once the accounting tailwind reverses.

Decision Impact

  • The best expression is not 'all AI stocks'. Rank by implied assumption first, then overlay allocation.
  • GPU quality is highest, but valuation pressure maps to trim-review at the name level, not a downgrade of the company.
  • Networking drops to avoid only on allocation, not valuation. Cloud stays, but at a lower weight.
  • Foundry is the only segment that does not lag on any of the three lists; it is the cleanest expression of the bottleneck.

What Would Change The View Upgrade / downgrade / review

Cooldown

Price momentum slows, inventory builds, lead times shorten; reinvestment rises while incremental returns flatten.
Stop-error
Thesis breaks
Revisions flatten while multiples expand, policy changes the serviceable market, or ROIIC stays below WACC for two years while reinvestment remains high.
A multiple is not a valuation until the assumption behind it is named. A capacity build is not value creation until ROIIC clears WACC.
