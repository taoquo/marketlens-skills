Nine tickers still look like diversification; the exposure does not. Five of nine require perpetual growth above the long-run nominal global rate, six reinvest more than 40% of operating cash flow, and the same AI capex, accounting conventions, and financing chain run across the group. The November de-rating helped the valuation leg — NVIDIA is now 16.7x, GOOGL 14-20x, META 19x — but it did not create independence. The portfolio is still risk-concentrated; the difference is that the risk now sits in cash conversion and governance, not only in the multiple.

Red Flags

  • The de-rating is not diversification: NVDA, TSM, AVGO, and AMD still share the same AI capex driver and the same customer budgets.
  • AI capex is increasingly debt-financed. A credit repricing hits the neocloud and sovereign customers before it hits the suppliers' income statements.
  • NVIDIA deployed $58.1B into equity securities in the first nine months and agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9B. Part of the ecosystem is financing its own demand.
  • Receivables and inventory are rising across the chain. If two or more holdings breach the same screen, treat it as one accounting practice, not two independent problems.
  • TSLA at 316-380x trailing earnings is a separate valuation risk, not an AI-capex hedge.

Decision Impact

  • Portfolio label: risk-concentrated. The de-rating lowers the valuation risk but not the shared-cash-flow risk.
  • NVDA moves from trim-review to hold/watch; TSM and MSFT are hold/watch; AVGO and AMD are monitor closely; GOOGL and META are hold/watch; TSLA is avoid or evidence-gap.
  • If the real portfolio's top 5 is above 50%, run a concentration review instead of adding another name.
  • Data confidence is capped at Medium because 4/9 implied assumptions are unmeasured; no weight changes are given.
